# Units and Readings

A unit groups related content inside a course — usually a week, a topic or a
chapter. Units hold assignments, readings and sometimes further units.

```
📖 Programming in Physics
├── ⚫ Week 1 — Introduction
│   ├── 📄 Lecture notes
│   ├── 🟦 Lab 1
│   └── 🟦 Homework 1
└── ⚫ Week 2 — Data Types
```

A unit is a circle, an assignment a square, and other material a document. The
colour is the **content type** your lecturer defined — lab, homework, exam,
project — so that the kind of work is visible at a glance. Which colour means
what is set per course; hovering tells you the type by name.

Units are not work of your own: there is nothing to commit or submit on a unit
row. What you can do is read what belongs to it.

## What a unit tells you

Hover over a unit for its title, how many items it contains, its type, and the
number of unread messages if there are any.

On the row itself you may see:

- `🔔 3` — unread messages in this unit
- `●` `↑` `⚠` — the state of the assignments underneath it, so a unit with
  uncommitted work is visible without expanding it. The marks mean the same
  everywhere; see [Courses](student-course.md#the-marks-on-a-row).

## Actions on a unit

### Show Description

Opens the text your lecturer wrote for this unit, rendered as a page. The entry
appears only when there is a description.

If your lecturer wrote it in more than one language, **Preview Description in
Another Language** lets you read the other versions. The one you get by default
follows the course language, with English as the fallback.

### Show Messages

Announcements and discussions for this unit — clarifications, hints, deadline
changes. Narrower than the course-wide messages, wider than one assignment's.

## Readings and other material

Not everything in a course is an assignment. Rows that are neither a unit nor an
assignment are material to read or use: lecture notes, references, data. They
have no repository, no tests and nothing to submit — so the actions on them are
limited to opening the description and reading messages.

If a row looks like something you should be handing in but offers no *Submit*,
it is one of these. Ask in the course messages if you expect otherwise; your
lecturer may still be preparing it.

## Hidden content

Your lecturer can hide a unit while it is being prepared, or after an exam. A
hidden unit simply does not appear for you — you never see an empty or greyed
row where one used to be. Content appearing later in the term is normal.
